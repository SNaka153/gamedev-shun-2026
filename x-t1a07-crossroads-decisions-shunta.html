    01 / Real decisions, visible results

    Each rule combines two booleans with AND (&&) or OR (||). NOT (!) flips a boolean. The first true rule wins, just like an if / else if / else chain. Conditions lower in the list are shown for learning, but their actions do not run.

    A number comparison such as myQueueNS > myQueueEW produces a boolean. This is the link between variables and decisions.

    Copy JS gives you a usable decision function. The page interprets the selected conditions; it never executes imported code.

    02 / A fictional traffic model

    Cars only travel straight. They queue before stop lines and follow at a fixed spacing. A phase holds for at least 5 seconds; pedestrians take 6.5 seconds. Direction changes use 1.2 seconds of amber and at least 1.2 seconds of all-red clearance. The next phase waits until committed traffic is clear.

    This interlock is separate from your rules. Even a bad policy cannot give conflicting greens. A poor policy can still create long queues or neglect pedestrians.

    This is a classroom simulation, not software for real signals. Vehicle sizes, speeds, timings and scoring are invented for this game.

    03 / Make a better policy

    Start with Adaptive city. Try changing OR to AND in the queue rule. Then try Always N–S and watch the opposite queue grow. Add a pedestrian request or ambulance and see which branch wins.

    Benchmarks use the current seed and demand, with a fresh start for each city. Manual events are excluded. Saving a report records the tested rules and results; saving rules records the editable setup.

    Space starts or pauses. Step +1s advances a paused city. If the school PC reduces motion, choose On for this page. Everything works offline; saves stay on your device.
